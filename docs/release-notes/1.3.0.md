In this release, rips and players time each track as a soundtrack album does, and a loop gets the new tags `loop_start` and `loop_length`. `usfrip` no longer takes titles or other tags from tables in its code. GoldenEye 007, Gex 64 and Paper Mario rip closer to the games. Rip every game again (see below). usf rips Nintendo 64 music into USF, the PSF-family format for the Nintendo 64, and plays it by running the game's code on an emulated console. The release comes with a ripper (`usfrip`), a renderer (`usfplay`), a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin.

## Rip every game again

Rips made with 1.2.3 or an earlier release have no loop tags, and their lengths follow the old rules. Their titles and their `game` tag come from the tables that this release removes. Rip every game again with this release to get the new lengths, tags and titles. The tracks of GoldenEye 007, Gex 64 and Paper Mario listed below also sound different. Sets made with earlier releases still play.

## Changes since 1.2.3

### Lengths like a soundtrack album's

- A track's `length` now counts from its first sound: the first sample that isn't 0, in either channel.
- A loop gets two passes and a fade of 7 s. In 1.2.3 its fade was 10 s. A loop also gets the tags `loop_start` and `loop_length`: the start of the loop and the length of one pass. A player can then play another number of passes.
- A track that ends gets its length at its last sound and a fade of 1 s, without the tail that it had after the sound.
- The length analysis of a track of music now runs for up to 3,600 s. In 1.2.3 it stopped at 600 s. A set of sound effects keeps the limit of 600 s. A track that reaches the limit gets 600 s and a fade of 7 s, and `usfrip` warns.
- A track whose profile sets its length and no fade ends at its last sound over -72 dB within that length. Super Mario 64's sound effects ended 0.5 s after their last sound. The measured loops, and Super Mario 64's held effects, fade over 7 s like other loops.
- `usfrip` prints each track's first sample and first sound on the track's line. A track that makes no sound gets no mini.
- A first sound that comes after a change of the output rate is now timed at the new rate. It came up to 1 ms early.

### Players

- The foobar2000 component, the XMPlay plugin and `usfplay` play each track as a soundtrack album plays it: 0.5 s of silence, the music from its first sound, then 1 s of silence.
- A fade now follows the square of the time left in it. Fades on most soundtrack albums have that curve. The curve is a setting, and `usfplay --fade-exponent P` sets it, from 0.5 to 4. A value of 1 gives the linear fade of 1.2.3.
- Eight new settings in the component and the plugin, and the matching options of `usfplay`, change how a track plays: the silence at the start, the silences before and after a track, the number of passes of a loop, one pass of a long loop, the fade of a loop, whether the length and fade tags count, and the silence at the end of a piece that ends.
- `usfplay --raw` renders a file as it is, with no skip and no added silence.
- In the XMPlay plugin and the component for 32-bit foobar2000, the square root of a double on the emulated CPU now follows the rounding mode that the game sets. Before, it always rounded to the nearest value. The 64-bit programs already followed the mode.

### Titles and tags

- `usfrip` no longer writes a game name, a year or a song title from a table in its code. The `game` tag is the value of `--game`, or else the name of the image or of the Aleck64 set, and the library is named after it. `--artist`, `--year` and `--copyright` set their tags.
- A track's title is the song's number in the game, such as `Sequence 0x2E` or `Song 0x00`. In Gex 64, the title is the track's place in the set, and in Super Mario 64's sound effects, the bank and number of the sound. When a set has several tracks of one song, each in a state that the game's code sets, the later ones add `, version 2`, `, version 3` and so on. The song names that earlier titles gave are gone.

### GoldenEye 007

- Surface 2 starts its stage music together with a wind background, and the set now has a track of the two together. That track takes the place of sequence 0x1C, as "Sequence 0x1C". Multiplayer plays sequence 0x1C alone, from its random list. That version is now the last track, "Sequence 0x1C, version 2".

### Gex 64

- A music update starts a song by setting up each of its channels. In the emulated console, the sound engine's tick could come in the middle of that start, and the channels set up after it missed the song's tempo, volume or reverb. Whether a tick splits a start is a matter of timing, not a state of the game. The ripper's driver now makes each update with interrupts off, and every track keeps the whole start.
- This changes tracks 0013 and 0024 to 0027. In track 0013, channels 5 to 12 ran 15.6 % fast until 10.7 s, and the first notes had no song volume or reverb. Track 0027 sounded from 0.10 s and now sounds from 0.35 s.
- Track 0022 starts its song during the level's load. The driver now makes that load with interrupts off as well. Before, channels 13 and 14 played at the default tempo until 8.79 s, and the track lasted 18.82 s rather than 17.55 s.

### Paper Mario

- A version of a song that sets no bus effect now keeps the effect of the song before it. The game does the same. Version 1 of song 0x79 now starts with the effect of Shiver City, and version 2 of song 0x83 with the effect of Dry Dry Desert.
- Raphael Raven's song gets tracks with the small room (effect 1), with the chorus (effect 3) and with no effect. Versions 1 to 3 of Normal Battle get tracks with no effect and with the chorus.

### Documentation

- The README now lists every tag that `usfrip` writes, and says what `--by` and `--no-length` do. It also corrects the length rule under "Known gaps" and credits the format's author. The third-party notices name the parts of the libraries that each program uses. The notes of earlier releases are reworded.
